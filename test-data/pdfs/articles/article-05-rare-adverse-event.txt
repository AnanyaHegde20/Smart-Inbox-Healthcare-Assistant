Drug-induced autoimmune hemolytic anemia associated with piperacillin-tazobactam: a case report and systematic review

Authors:
Elena Petrov, MD^1^
James Wilson, MD^2^
Li Wei, MD, PhD^3^

^1^ Department of Hematology, National University Hospital
^2^ Department of Internal Medicine, City Medical Center
^3^ Division of Clinical Pharmacology, University Hospital

Corresponding author: e.petrov@nuh.edu.sg

Abstract

Background: Piperacillin-tazobactam is a widely used broad-spectrum antibiotic. While hepatotoxicity and nephrotoxicity are well-documented, drug-induced autoimmune hemolytic anemia (AIHA) is a rare and potentially life-threatening adverse event with limited published data.

Case presentation: We present the case of a 62-year-old Caucasian man who developed severe AIHA after 10 days of piperacillin-tazobactam therapy for hospital-acquired pneumonia. The patient presented with fatigue, jaundice, and dark urine. Laboratory investigations revealed hemoglobin 6.2 g/dL, reticulocyte count 12.5%, direct antiglobulin test (DAT) positive for IgG and C3d, and elevated lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) 1,250 U/L. Piperacillin-tazobactam was discontinued, and the patient was treated with corticosteroids. Hemoglobin normalized over 4 weeks. A systematic review identified 23 previously reported cases, with a mortality rate of 8.7%.

Conclusions: AIHA should be considered in patients receiving piperacillin-tazobactam who develop unexplained anemia. Early recognition and drug discontinuation are critical for favorable outcomes.

Keywords: piperacillin-tazobactam, drug-induced autoimmune hemolytic anemia, direct antiglobulin test, case report

INTRODUCTION

Drug-induced autoimmune hemolytic anemia (AIHA) is a rare adverse drug reaction with an estimated incidence of 1 in 100,000 drug exposures. The condition occurs when drugs modify red blood cell surface antigens, leading to autoantibody production and subsequent hemolysis.

Piperacillin-tazobactam is one of the most commonly used broad-spectrum antibiotics in hospital settings. While immune-mediated hemolytic anemia has been reported with other beta-lactam antibiotics, reports with piperacillin-tazobactam are limited to case reports and small series.

We present a case of severe AIHA associated with piperacillin-tazobactam and provide a systematic review of published literature.

CASE PRESENTATION

Patient:
A 62-year-old Caucasian man (weight 78 kg, height 175 cm) was admitted to the medical ICU with hospital-acquired pneumonia.

Medical History:
- Type 2 diabetes mellitus (metformin 1000mg BID)
- Hypertension (amlodipine 5mg daily)
- No history of autoimmune disease
- No prior blood transfusions
- No known drug allergies

Medications on Admission:
1. Metformin 1000mg PO BID
2. Amlodipine 5mg PO daily
3. Insulin glargine 20 units SC at bedtime

Hospital Course:
Day 1-3: Patient treated with piperacillin-tazobactam 4.5g IV every 6 hours for hospital-acquired pneumonia. Clinical improvement noted by Day 3.

Day 10: Patient developed progressive fatigue, jaundice, and dark urine. No fever, rash, or lymphadenopathy.

PHYSICAL EXAMINATION (Day 10):
Vital Signs: BP 112/68, HR 102, RR 18, Temp 36.8°C, SpO2 96% on room air
General: Alert, oriented, moderate jaundice
HEENT: Scleral icterus, oral mucosa pale
Cardiovascular: Regular rate, no murmurs
Respiratory: Clear to bilateral, decreased breath sounds at bases
Abdomen: Soft, non-tender, liver palpable 2cm below costal margin
Extremities: No edema, warm and well-perfused
Skin: Jaundiced, no rash, no petechiae

LABORATORY INVESTIGATIONS (Day 10):
Hemoglobin: 6.2 g/dL (baseline 13.8 g/dL on admission)
Hematocrit: 18.6%
WBC: 11.2 x 10^9/L
Platelets: 285 x 10^9/L
Reticulocyte count: 12.5% (elevated)
LDH: 1,250 U/L (elevated, normal <250)
Total bilirubin: 4.8 mg/dL (elevated, normal <1.2)
Direct bilirubin: 0.9 mg/dL
Haptoglobin: <10 mg/dL (undetectable)
Direct antiglobulin test (DAT): Positive for IgG and C3d
Iron studies: Ferritin 450 ng/mL, TIBC 180 mcg/dL
Peripheral smear: Spherocytes present, no schistocytes

DIAGNOSIS:
Drug-induced autoimmune hemolytic anemia (AIHA) secondary to piperacillin-tazobactam

Differential Diagnosis Considered:
1. Autoimmune hemolytic anemia (primary)
2. Microangiopathic hemolytic anemia (excluded - no schistocytes)
3. Infection-related hemolysis
4. Other drug-induced hemolysis

MANAGEMENT:

Day 10: Piperacillin-tazobactam discontinued immediately
Alternative antibiotic: Meropenem 1g IV every 8 hours initiated

Day 11: Prednisone 1 mg/kg/day initiated (60mg daily)
Transfusion consideration: Not initiated (hemoglobin stable, reticulocyte response adequate)

Day 14: Hemoglobin 7.1 g/dL (improving)
Day 21: Hemoglobin 9.4 g/dL
Day 28: Hemoglobin 11.8 g/dL

Corticosteroid taper:
- Weeks 1-2: 60mg daily
- Weeks 3-4: 40mg daily
- Weeks 5-6: 20mg daily
- Weeks 7-8: 10mg daily then discontinuation

Outcome at 3 months:
- Hemoglobin: 13.2 g/dL
- DAT: Weakly positive for C3d (IgG negative)
- No recurrence of hemolysis
- Patient completed antibiotic course for pneumonia with full recovery

SYSTEMATIC REVIEW:

Search Strategy:
PubMed, Embase, and Cochrane Library searched from inception to December 2024.
Search terms: "piperacillin" OR "tazobactam" OR "piperacillin-tazobactam" AND "hemolytic anemia" OR "autoimmune hemolytic anemia"

Results:
23 previously reported cases identified (1985-2024)

DISCUSSION:

This case and systematic review highlight several important points:

1. TIMING: AIHA typically develops 5-21 days after starting piperacillin-tazobactam, consistent with immune-mediated mechanisms.

2. SEVERITY: Hemoglobin drops significantly (mean 7 g/dL), requiring close monitoring and potential transfusion support.

3. DIAGNOSIS: DAT positivity is essential for diagnosis. Both IgG and C3d may be positive, indicating complement involvement.

4. MANAGEMENT: Drug discontinuation is the cornerstone of treatment. Corticosteroids accelerate recovery. Transfusion is reserved for severe cases.

5. OUTCOMES: Prognosis is generally favorable with early recognition and appropriate management. Mortality is low (8.7%) and mostly in patients with significant comorbidities.

CONCLUSIONS:

Healthcare providers should maintain awareness of AIHA as a rare but important adverse effect of piperacillin-tazobactam. Unexplained anemia during therapy should prompt DAT testing and consideration of drug discontinuation. Early recognition and intervention are associated with favorable outcomes.
