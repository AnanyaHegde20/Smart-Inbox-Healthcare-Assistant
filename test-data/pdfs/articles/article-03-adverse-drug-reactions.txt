Incidence and Predictors of Adverse Drug Reactions in Hospitalized Elderly Patients: A Prospective Cohort Study

ABSTRACT

Background: Adverse drug reactions (ADRs) are a leading cause of hospital admissions and prolonged stays in elderly patients. This study aimed to determine the incidence, severity, and predictors of ADRs in hospitalized patients aged ≥65 years.

Methods: We conducted a prospective cohort study in three teaching hospitals over 12 months (2024-2025). Patients aged ≥65 years admitted for ≥48 hours were included. ADRs were identified through daily clinical review, laboratory monitoring, and patient interviews. ADR causality was assessed using the Naranjo algorithm.

Results: Of 2,847 patients enrolled (mean age 74.3 ± 6.8 years; 54% female), 487 (17.1%) experienced at least one ADR during their hospital stay. The incidence rate was 22.4 ADRs per 100 patient-days. Most ADRs were classified as possible (62%) or probable (28%). Serious ADRs occurred in 89 patients (3.1%). Independent predictors included polypharmacy (OR 2.8), renal impairment (OR 2.1), history of ADR (OR 3.4), and hepatic dysfunction (OR 1.9).

Conclusions: ADRs are common in hospitalized elderly patients and are largely predictable. Medication review targeting high-risk patients may reduce ADR incidence.

Keywords: adverse drug reactions, elderly, polypharmacy, hospital, epidemiology

INTRODUCTION

Adverse drug reactions (ADRs) represent a significant cause of morbidity and mortality in healthcare settings. In elderly patients, the risk of ADRs is particularly elevated due to age-related physiological changes, polypharmacy, and comorbidities.

Previous studies have reported ADR incidence rates of 10-30% in hospitalized elderly patients, but methodological differences make comparisons difficult. Furthermore, most studies have been conducted in single centers, limiting generalizability.

This multi-center prospective study aimed to:
1. Determine the incidence and spectrum of ADRs in hospitalized elderly patients
2. Identify modifiable and non-modifiable risk factors
3. Assess the impact of ADRs on clinical outcomes
4. Develop a practical risk stratification tool

METHODS

Study Design:
Prospective cohort study conducted at:
- London Royal Hospital (n=1,245)
- Manchester General Hospital (n=987)
- Bristol Royal Infirmary (n=615)

Study Period: January 2024 - December 2025

Inclusion Criteria:
- Age ≥65 years
- Hospital stay ≥48 hours
- At least one medication prescribed during admission
- Informed consent obtained

Exclusion Criteria:
- Palliative care patients
- Patients admitted for ADR (to avoid selection bias)
- Inability to provide informed consent

DISCUSSION

This multi-center study found that 17.1% of hospitalized elderly patients experienced at least one ADR, with an incidence rate of 22.4 per 100 patient-days. This is consistent with previous reports, though the multi-center design enhances generalizability.

The most common ADRs were acute kidney injury (18.3%), hypoglycemia (13.8%), and bleeding (11.1%). These findings align with known pharmacological effects of commonly prescribed medications in elderly patients.

Risk Stratification Model:

Based on our findings, we propose a simple risk stratification tool:

LOW RISK (0-2 points):
- No polypharmacy (0)
- No ADR history (0)
- Normal renal function (0)
- Age <80 (0)
+ Age ≥80 (1 point)
+ History of ADR (1 point)
+ Renal impairment (1 point)

MODERATE RISK (3-4 points):
Enhanced monitoring recommended
- Daily clinical review
- Medication reconciliation by pharmacist
- Renal function monitoring

HIGH RISK (≥5 points):
Maximum surveillance
- Twice-daily clinical review
- Pharmacist-led medication management
- Therapeutic drug monitoring
- Weekly comprehensive medication review

LIMITATIONS

1. Single-country study (UK) - may not generalize to other healthcare systems
2. ADR assessment subject to observer bias despite standardized tools
3. Some ADRs may have been missed despite comprehensive surveillance
4. Long-term outcomes beyond 30 days were not assessed
5. Genetic testing for CYP2D6 status was not performed on all patients

=== PAGE 10 ===
CONCLUSIONS

ADRs affect approximately 1 in 6 hospitalized elderly patients and are associated with significant morbidity, prolonged hospital stays, and increased costs. Most ADRs are predictable and potentially preventable through:

1. Comprehensive medication review on admission
2. Renal dose adjustments
3. Monitoring of high-alert medications
4. Use of clinical decision support tools
5. Patient education about warning signs

Future research should focus on developing and validating automated ADR prediction models using electronic health record data.
