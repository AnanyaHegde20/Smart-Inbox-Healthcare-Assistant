Medication Reconciliation in Hospitalized Patients: 2025 Updated Recommendations

Authors:
Robert Chen, PharmD, BCPS^1^

^1^ Department of Pharmacy, Riverside Medical Center

Published in: Journal of Clinical Pharmacy and Therapeutics
Volume 51, Issue 2, Pages 89-102 (2025)

Abstract:
Medication reconciliation is a critical process for preventing medication errors during care transitions. This guideline provides updated recommendations based on systematic evidence review and expert consensus. Key changes from the 2021 edition include integration of electronic health record-based reconciliation tools, patient engagement strategies, and outcome measurement frameworks.

Recommendations are categorized using the GRADE system:
- Strong recommendation (Grade A)
- Moderate recommendation (Grade B)
- Weak recommendation (Grade C)
- Best practice statement (Grade D)

=== PAGE 2 ===
INTRODUCTION

Medication discrepancies occur in 20-70% of patients during care transitions, with approximately 30% of these discrepancies having potential to cause harm. The Joint Commission requires medication reconciliation as a National Patient Safety Goal.

This guideline updates the 2021 ACCP recommendations with new evidence and addresses emerging challenges including:
- Integration with electronic health records
- Telehealth transitions of care
- Patient engagement in reconciliation processes
- Resource-limited settings

SCOPE:
This guideline applies to all healthcare settings where medication reconciliation is performed, including:
- Acute care hospitals
- Ambulatory care clinics
- Long-term care facilities
- Transition of care settings

=== PAGE 3 ===
SUMMARY OF RECOMMENDATIONS

DOMAIN 1: TIMING OF RECONCILIATION

Recommendation 1.1: Medication reconciliation should be performed at every transition of care (Grade A)
- Admission to hospital
- Transfer between units
- Discharge from hospital

Recommendation 1.2: Reconciliation should be completed within 24 hours of admission (Grade B)
- For complex patients (>10 medications): within 48 hours with documented rationale

Recommendation 1.3: Discharge reconciliation should be completed before medication dispensing (Grade A)

DOMAIN 2: PERSONNEL

Recommendation 2.1: Pharmacist involvement in medication reconciliation is recommended (Grade A)
- Pharmacist-led reconciliation reduces discrepancies by 50-70%

Recommendation 2.2: Nursing staff should perform initial medication history (Grade B)
- Pharmacist verification recommended for high-risk patients

Recommendation 2.3: Physician review and authorization of final medication list is required (Grade A)

DOMAIN 3: INFORMATION SOURCES

Recommendation 3.1: Multiple sources should be used for medication history (Grade A)
- Patient interview
- Prescription bottles
- Pharmacy records
- Prior medical records
- Caregiver input

Recommendation 3.2: Electronic health record medication lists should be reconciled with patient-reported medications (Grade B)

=== PAGE 4 ===
DOMAIN 4: DOCUMENTATION

Recommendation 4.1: Discrepancies should be documented with resolution rationale (Grade A)
- Type of discrepancy
- Action taken
- Responsible provider

Recommendation 4.2: The reconciled medication list should be provided to the patient (Grade A)
- Written format preferred
- Patient's health literacy level considered

Recommendation 4.3: Post-discharge medication lists should be sent to outpatient providers (Grade B)

DOMAIN 5: PATIENT ENGAGEMENT

Recommendation 5.1: Patients should be actively involved in reconciliation process (Grade A)
- Teach-back method recommended
- Medication list provided in patient-friendly format

Recommendation 5.2: Caregiver involvement should be assessed and incorporated when appropriate (Grade B)

DOMAIN 6: TECHNOLOGY

Recommendation 6.1: Electronic health record-based reconciliation tools are recommended (Grade B)
- Automated discrepancy detection
- Clinical decision support
- Integration with community pharmacy records

Recommendation 6.2: Telehealth reconciliation is acceptable for appropriate patients (Grade C)
- Visual confirmation of medications recommended
- Photo documentation of medication bottles

=== PAGE 5 ===
IMPLEMENTATION FRAMEWORK

Step 1: Assessment
- Identify patient population and care transitions
- Assess available resources and technology
- Determine reconciliation model (pharmacist-led vs. interdisciplinary)

Step 2: Process Design
- Develop standardized workflows
- Create documentation templates
- Establish escalation procedures for discrepancies

Step 3: Training
- Initial training for all reconciliation personnel
- Competency assessment
- Ongoing education program

Step 4: Technology Integration
- EHR configuration
- Decision support rules
- Reporting dashboards

Step 5: Monitoring and Evaluation
- Discrepancy tracking
- Outcome measurement
- Quality improvement cycles

OUTCOME MEASURES:
| Measure | Target | Data Source |
|---------|--------|-------------|
| Reconciliation completion rate | >95% | EHR |
| Discrepancy rate | <10% | Reconciliation records |
| Adverse drug event rate | 25% reduction | Incident reports |
| Patient satisfaction | >4/5 | Survey |
| Readmission rate | 15% reduction | Administrative data |

=== PAGE 6 ===
QUALITY IMPROVEMENT CONSIDERATIONS

Common Barriers and Solutions:

Barrier: Insufficient pharmacist staffing
Solution: Prioritize high-risk patients, utilize pharmacy technicians for medication history

Barrier: Time constraints
Solution: Streamline workflows, use technology to automate documentation

Barrier: Incomplete information sources
Solution: Establish community pharmacy partnerships, patient engagement

Barrier: Communication gaps
Solution: Standardized handoff tools, closed-loop communication systems

APPENDIX A: High-Risk Patient Criteria
- >10 medications
- High-alert medications (anticoagulants, insulin, opioids)
- History of adverse drug events
- Cognitive impairment
- Limited English proficiency
- Multiple care transitions in past 90 days
