Abstract:
Background: Falls remain one of the most common patient safety events in acute care hospitals, with rates ranging from 2.2 to 8.3 falls per 1,000 patient days. This review synthesizes current evidence on modifiable and non-modifiable risk factors and evaluates prevention interventions.

Methods: We conducted a systematic search of PubMed, CINAHL, and Cochrane Library for studies published between 2018-2024. Included studies evaluated fall prevention interventions in adult inpatient settings.

Results: Of 247 articles screened, 42 met inclusion criteria. Key modifiable risk factors included: medication effects (OR 2.3, 95% CI 1.8-2.9), mobility impairment (OR 3.1, 95% CI 2.4-3.9), and environmental hazards (OR 1.7, 95% CI 1.3-2.2). Multifactorial interventions showed greatest effectiveness (RR 0.62, 95% CI 0.51-0.76).

Conclusions: Evidence supports multifactorial fall prevention programs combining medication review, environmental modifications, and patient education. Single-intervention approaches are insufficient.

Keywords: falls, patient safety, acute care, prevention, risk factors

INTRODUCTION

Falls in hospitals are associated with increased length of stay, higher healthcare costs, and significant patient morbidity. The Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS) classifies falls with injury as "never events," and hospitals are no longer reimbursed for costs associated with fall-related injuries.

Previous reviews have focused primarily on elderly populations in long-term care settings. However, falls occur across all age groups in acute care, and prevention strategies must be adaptable to diverse patient populations.

This review aimed to:
1. Identify current evidence on fall risk factors in acute care
2. Evaluate effectiveness of prevention interventions
3. Provide practical recommendations for clinical implementation

METHODS

Search Strategy:
Databases searched: PubMed, CINAHL, Cochrane Library
Search terms: ("patient fall*" OR "inpatient fall*" OR "hospital fall*") AND ("prevention" OR "risk factor*" OR "intervention")
Limits: English language, adult (19+ years), published 2018-2024

Inclusion Criteria:
- Study design: RCT, quasi-experimental, cohort, case-control
- Setting: Acute care hospitals
- Population: Adult inpatients
- Outcome: Fall incidence or fall risk factors

DISCUSSION

Our findings support the implementation of comprehensive fall prevention programs that address multiple risk factors simultaneously. The evidence is particularly strong for:

1. MEDICATION MANAGEMENT
Medication review by pharmacists, particularly targeting sedatives, antihypertensives, and opioids, showed consistent reductions in fall rates. The optimal approach appears to be pharmacist-led review within 48 hours of admission, with recommendations communicated to the prescribing team.

2. MOBILITY ASSESSMENT AND INTERVENTION
Early mobilization programs, even for patients on bed rest, showed significant fall reduction. Key elements included:
- Structured mobility protocols
- Progressive mobility goals
- Assistive device assessment
- Strength and balance exercises

3. ENVIRONMENTAL MODIFICATIONS
Lighting improvements, call bell placement, and bed/chair alarm systems showed modest but consistent effects. The most effective environmental interventions were those that were consistently maintained rather than one-time modifications.

4. PATIENT EDUCATION
Patient and family education about fall risk was most effective when delivered verbally with written reinforcement. Technology-based education (tablets, apps) showed promising results in younger populations.

LIMITATIONS
- Heterogeneity in outcome measures limited meta-analysis
- Most studies were conducted in North America and Europe
- Long-term sustainability of interventions was rarely assessed
- Cost-effectiveness data were limited

=== PAGE 8 ===
CONCLUSIONS AND CLINICAL IMPLICATIONS

This review supports the following evidence-based recommendations:

1. Implement multifactorial fall prevention programs (Strong evidence)
2. Conduct pharmacist-led medication review within 48 hours (Strong evidence)
3. Initiate early mobilization protocols (Strong evidence)
4. Ensure consistent environmental safety measures (Moderate evidence)
5. Provide patient and family education (Moderate evidence)
6. Use validated fall risk assessment tools (Strong evidence)

Future research should focus on:
- Sustainability of fall prevention programs
- Cost-effectiveness analyses
- Implementation in diverse healthcare settings
- Role of technology in fall prevention
